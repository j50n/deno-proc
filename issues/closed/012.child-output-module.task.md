# A child's stdout as one module

The reading of a child's stdout is spread over `pipeReader`, an on-exit handler
in the `Process` constructor, four fields and `output()`. Three fixes in two
releases landed in that coordination, the 0.28.1 crash (a stream already locked
by someone else) among them, and it can only be tested through shells with
grandchildren. From 008.

Decided: one internal module that takes the stream and owns the reading, the
drain at exit, the quiet cutoff after a timeout and the locked check, with a
small interface (read it once; the child exited, timed out or not). `Process`
keeps only the calls.

Must not change: no file left open by awaiting only `status`; output still
readable after `status`; `timeoutMs` not held by a grandchild; reading
`process.stdout` directly works; an early stop returns at once; `main` waits for
readers after a signal.

Check: the suite (third-review tests in particular); new tests of the module on
plain `ReadableStream`s for each behavior above; the fd probe.

Decided: a class, `ChildOutput`, with `read()` and `exited(timedOut)`;
`trackReading` stays in `Process.output()`, so the module knows nothing of
shutdown. Its doc comment carries the whys that were spread over the constructor
and `pipeReader`.

Closed by aa02203.

Verified: `tests/child-output.test.ts`, seven tests on plain streams (every
chunk to the end; read at the exit and kept for a later reader; that read stops
at a pause; a stream locked elsewhere left alone, which fails if the locked
check is removed; a waiting reader let go after a timeout; no cutoff without
one; an early stop cancels the stream). Full suite 632 passed, third-review and
shutdown tests among them. By hand against real children: 1,000 `.status` calls
leave no files open, output read after `status`, a timed-out shell with a
grandchild fails in ~0.4 s, `process.stdout` read directly, a failing command's
log kept, SIGTERM under `main` keeps the child's last words (89 lines).
